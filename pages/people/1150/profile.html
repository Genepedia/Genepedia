John Henry Davis

John Henry Davis was a Adventurous prospector who came to Johannesburg to find gold on the Rand, he came form Paarl in the Cape of Good Hope Colony. He was a ship owner and a gentleman of versatile character. He lost three ships in the Napoleonic wars and travelled extensively, he spoke seven languages fluently and obtained a considerable knowledge of geology – particularly in regard to auriferous and diamondiferous deposits in Brazil, He was also an authority on astronomy, on which subject he was accustomed to lecture, He also devised a new form of compass. Discovery of Fold on the Witwatersrand, an unrecorded incident” August 31, 1905, page 124 “In the year 1852, just about the time the Zand River Treaty was signed, an English man named John Henry Davis Traveled through the Southern Transvaal in search of precious metals. Davis had studied Mineralogy and could identify any valuable minerals at sight. He was accompanied by his second son, a youngster about 10 years of age. At that time this country was a wilderness, inhabited by a few farmers, who lived principally by hunting. Davis Sojourned amongst the farmers for a couple of years, searching for gold. During his stay on the Witwatersrand he lived with a certain Mr Pretorius on the farm Paardekraal, now Krugersdorp, and there he found gold in considerable quantities. It will still be fresh in the memory of the diggers of 1887 that alluvial gold was found on Witpoortjie, and in all probability it was from this patch that Davis procured his specimens. Andries Pretorius was President of the Transvaal at that time, and the seat of the Government was at Potchefstroom. Thither Davis journeyed with his treasure and exhibited it to the President, who immediately on seeing the yellow metal realized the danger his people would be in if the occurrence of gold was made known to the world, and he instructed the Treasurer to purchase the gold from Davis, and ordered the old man out of the country. This is the story as told by Mr. Draper himself. Later on, in about 1873, Mrs. Meyer, living in Utrecht, gave Draper the details of Davis’s visit to the President of Potchefstroom, and advised Draper to search on the Witwatersrand for gold, as Davis had told her that where he had found his “collection” there was plenty more.
